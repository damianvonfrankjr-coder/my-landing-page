Expert reserve studies

A Reserve Study is a financial guide that helps your community budget for the major repairs and replacements of common-area components — roofs, pavement, mechanical systems, and more. Here is how ours work, level by level.

Request a proposal View sample reports

Why it matters

The same projects come due and the same costs are incurred whether you have a Reserve Study or not. Knowing what’s coming lets you prepare — instead of facing a surprise special assessment. Our goal is to eliminate those surprises.

Proactive planning saves the “urgency” upcharge vendors add during emergency failures, avoids loan interest, and keeps property values higher. And a funding plan adopted by all stakeholders — boards, committees, managers, staff — builds trust within the community.

The risks of looking the other way
Excessive maintenance costs
Emergency repairs and replacements
Financial surprises and strain
Decreased property values

The four engagement levels

Every level ends the same way: a detailed 30-year financial analysis guiding your long-term funding plan.

Level Engagement What happens
1 Full reserve study On-site inspection of all common assets and amenities · condition assessment and lifespan estimation · replacement-cost forecasting · full 30-year financial analysis.
2 Update, with site visit For associations refreshing an existing study. New site inspection to assess current asset conditions · market-based cost updates · refreshed 30-year analysis.
3 Update, no site visit We analyze existing data and client documentation to update costs and projections — available when the prior report is recent and no reserve projects have occurred.
4 New development, not yet built Plan analysis from construction documents · component assessment · cost forecasting by construction material · a funding plan for the future association.

The process

From the first walk of the property to the final revision, every engagement follows the same disciplined sequence — and you stay in the loop throughout, including a 60-day revision period after delivery.

Common questions, answered →

  1. Inspect

    Site inspection and physical analysis; create or update the component inventory.

  2. Estimate

    Project life and cost estimates, project prioritization, and follow-up research with you and your vendors.

  3. Model

    Review of current budget and funding, funding recommendations, and cash-flow optimization.

  4. Deliver

    Report provided for client review, then collaboration and refinement during the revision period.

What the report gives you

The component list. A comprehensive inventory of common major assets: location, visual condition, specifics like the make, model, and manufacture year of your HVAC system, maintenance recommendations, and cost-estimate ranges.

Your current funding position. Using current-year funding and the estimated year-end reserve balance, we determine the strength of your existing approach.

A strategic funding plan. With what you have and where you stand established, a funding strategy catered to long-term sustainability — no surprises.
